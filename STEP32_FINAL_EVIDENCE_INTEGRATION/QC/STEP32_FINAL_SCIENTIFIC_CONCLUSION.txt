Integrated analysis supports D166V as a plausible function-altering PNPLA3 variant based on convergent computational evidence. The Asp166→Val substitution changes the chemical identity and local interaction environment of residue 166, while producing negligible global backbone displacement. Independent structural analysis reproduces the local remodeling signal. Independent biological literature establishes that PNPLA3 participates in hepatic lipid metabolism and that PNPLA3 genetic variation is strongly associated with steatotic liver disease. The computational D166V mechanism is therefore biologically consistent with the known PNPLA3/MASLD framework. The therapeutic analysis further identifies a residue-166-centered pocket and computationally prioritizes candidate ligands, with CID 71236597 showing a D166V-preferred docking score. However, the evidence does not establish that D166V causes MASLD, does not demonstrate a measured loss or gain of PNPLA3 enzymatic function, and does not demonstrate therapeutic rescue. The principal unresolved scientific gap is therefore variant-specific experimental validation rather than absence of a computational mechanistic hypothesis.
